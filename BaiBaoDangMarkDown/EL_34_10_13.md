# Degradation-Aware Restoration and Quality-Calibrated Object Detection in Hazy and Noisy Environments 

Zihao Wang, Xiaolin Zhang*, Ran Liu 

**_Abstract_ —Object detection in haze-degraded environments is challenging because haze reduces visibility, weakens target texture and boundary cues, and increases background interference. Consequently, DETR-based detectors may exhibit inadequate representation of degraded inputs, imprecise bounding-box localization, and misalignment between classification confidence and localization quality. To address these issues, we propose DARQ-Det, an end-to-end object detector for haze-degraded scenes. The Degradation-Aware Restoration Module enhances degraded inputs through lightweight residual compensation and total variation regularization, thereby improving the discriminability of target regions. The Hierarchical IoU-Focused Refinement Module introduces IoU-aware focal reweighting to place greater emphasis on low-quality matched boxes during regression optimization. The IoU Quality Calibration Module estimates the localization quality of candidate boxes and combines it with classification confidence to improve candidate ranking. Experiments are conducted on two synthetic haze-degraded remote sensing datasets, RSOD-Haze and HRSC2016-Haze, and the real-world hazy dataset RTTS. DARQ-Det obtains mAP scores of 68.13%, 91.62%, and 60.75% on RSOD-Haze, HRSC2016-Haze, and RTTS, respectively. The results indicate consistent improvements over the RF-DETR baseline in detection accuracy and localization quality across synthetic and real haze-degraded data.** 

**_Index Terms_ —Haze-degraded object detection, remote sensing object detection, image restoration, IoU quality calibration, DETR.** 

## I. INTRODUCTION 

**I** Nsubstantially.recent years,Both conventionalobject detectiondetectionhasframeworksprogressed[1] and end-to-end detectors represented by DETR [2] have achieved strong performance under standard imaging conditions. Their performance may nevertheless deteriorate in remote sensing scenes affected by haze. Targets in remote sensing images are often small, densely distributed, and embedded in complex backgrounds, while haze further reduces contrast, weakens texture and boundary cues, and increases background interference. These factors make feature representation and precise localization more difficult. 

Reliable detection in haze-degraded scenes requires both robust classification and accurate localization. This capability is relevant to applications such as remote sensing monitoring, ship detection, and traffic-scene analysis. Sun 

and Tian [3] reported that traffic-sign detection remains difficult in complex road environments, underscoring the broader need for detectors that remain reliable under degraded visual conditions. Despite recent advances in feature modeling and inference efficiency, haze can still produce three related limitations: inadequate representation of degraded inputs, inaccurate bounding-box localization, and inconsistency between classification confidence and localization quality. RF-DETR [4] offers a suitable baseline because it combines effective backbone representation with a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off. 

To address these limitations, we propose DARQ-Det, an end-to-end object detection framework that integrates input restoration, localization refinement, and quality calibration. DARQ-Det contains three complementary components. The Degradation-Aware Restoration Module (DARM) applies lightweight residual compensation with total variation regularization to improve the representation of haze-degraded inputs. The Hierarchical IoU-Focused Refinement Module (HIFRM) introduces IoU-aware focal reweighting into bounding-box regression to emphasize low-quality matched boxes. The IoU Quality Calibration Module (IQCM) estimates the localization quality of candidate boxes and uses this estimate to calibrate classification confidence. Together, these components form a progressive pipeline from degraded-input compensation to localization refinement and score calibration. 

The main contributions are summarized as follows: 

(1) We propose DARQ-Det, an end-to-end object detection framework for haze-degraded scenes that jointly addresses degraded-input representation, bounding-box localization, and prediction-score calibration. 

(2) We develop DARM, which combines lightweight residual compensation with total variation regularization to improve the representation of haze-degraded images. 

(3) We introduce HIFRM, which incorporates IoU-aware focal reweighting into bounding-box regression to place greater emphasis on low-quality matched boxes. 

(4) We design IQCM to estimate localization quality and combine it with classification confidence, thereby improving candidate ranking and the reliability of the final detections.

Object detection methods can be broadly grouped into conventional non-DETR approaches [1] and DETR-based approaches [2]. Non-DETR methods include two-stage detectors [1] and one-stage detectors [5], with subsequent

research improving their accuracy, feature representation, and inference efficiency. Hou et al. [6] investigated lightweight object detection for remote sensing images, while Zhang et al. [7] developed POD-YOLO with bidirectional dynamic cross-level feature aggregation. These studies indicate that efficient and discriminative feature representation remains important, particularly in complex scenes and resource-constrained settings. 

Faster R-CNN is a representative two-stage detector that incorporates a Region Proposal Network into the detection pipeline. The proposal and detection stages share convolutional features, allowing region generation, object classification, and bounding-box regression to be optimized within a unified framework. In parallel, advances in visual backbones have further improved the quality of representations used by detection models. 

Swin Transformer [8] constructs hierarchical feature representations and uses shifted-window self-attention to exchange information across local windows while controlling computational cost. This design makes Transformer backbones suitable for dense prediction tasks such as object detection. However, many conventional detection frameworks continue to rely on anchor design and post-processing, which increases pipeline complexity and motivates the development of end-to-end alternatives. 

## _B. DETR-Based End-to-End Detection and Real-Time Advances_ 

DETR [2] formulates object detection as a set-prediction problem and uses bipartite matching to produce detections without anchor design or non-maximum suppression. Subsequent studies have focused primarily on training convergence, query initialization, detection accuracy, and real-time inference. 

DINO [9] improves DETR training and detection performance through contrastive denoising, mixed query selection, and a two-stage look-forward mechanism. Its design primarily emphasizes detection accuracy, with less emphasis on real-time deployment. RT-DETR [10] addresses real-time end-to-end detection through an efficient hybrid encoder and an improved query-initialization strategy. RT-DETRv2 [11] further improves training flexibility and deployment practicality. Nevertheless, efficient representation modeling and lightweight architectural design remain relevant concerns for real-time DETR variants. 

LW-DETR [12] adopts a compact encoder–decoder architecture to balance detection accuracy and real-time performance. Building on this line of work, RF-DETR [4] combines a DINOv2 backbone with weight-sharing neural architecture search to improve feature representation and the accuracy–latency trade-off. These properties make RF-DETR an appropriate baseline for studying input restoration, localization refinement, and quality calibration in haze-degraded scenes. 

_C. Object Detection under Haze and Other Visual Degradations_ 

Visual degradations such as haze and low illumination reduce visibility and obscure image details, thereby increasing 

the difficulty of object detection. Earlier studies commonly applied image enhancement or dehazing as a preprocessing step. Because restoration and detection are optimized separately in such pipelines, the restored images are not necessarily aligned with the detector’s objectives. 

IA-YOLO [13] integrates image enhancement with object detection and optimizes both components jointly. The method is primarily designed for natural scenes and does not specifically address the characteristics of haze-degraded remote sensing imagery. For remote sensing applications, DHC-Net [14] combines adaptive dehazing with contrastive learning to mitigate image degradation and class imbalance. 

Existing work has mainly examined front-end restoration, feature enhancement, or their integration with conventional detectors. Comparatively less attention has been given to adapting end-to-end DETR frameworks to haze-degraded scenes. We therefore use RF-DETR as the baseline and incorporate input restoration, localization refinement, and quality calibration within a unified end-to-end framework. 

## _D. Localization Optimization and Quality Calibration_ 

Beyond degraded feature representation, detection performance in hazy scenes is also affected by imprecise bounding-box localization and misalignment between classification confidence and localization quality. DETR-based methods commonly use _L_ 1 and GIoU losses [15] for box regression. Although this formulation is broadly applicable, it does not explicitly differentiate matched boxes according to localization difficulty. Under haze, blurred contours and increased background interference can make ambiguous matches more difficult to refine. 

Previous studies have incorporated IoU information into classification objectives or ranking strategies to reduce classification–localization misalignment. For example, LW-DETR [12] uses an IoU-aware classification loss so that the classification objective reflects localization quality during training. This approach suggests that candidate ranking based solely on classification confidence may be suboptimal and that explicit quality information can improve ranking reliability. 

Although these approaches improve box regression or classification–localization consistency, most are developed for general object detection and do not explicitly consider haze-induced degradation. Accordingly, we introduce targeted modifications to RF-DETR for localization refinement and quality calibration in haze-degraded scenes.

## IV. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN AND IMPLEMENTATION 

## _A. Dataset and Haze Construction_ 

DARQ-Det is evaluated on three datasets: the synthetic haze-degraded remote sensing datasets RSOD-Haze and HRSC2016-Haze, and the real-world hazy dataset RTTS. The first two datasets are generated offline from RSOD and HRSC2016, respectively, while RTTS is used to assess performance under naturally occurring haze. 

**RSOD** [18]. RSOD is a public remote sensing object detection dataset containing 976 images and 6,950 annotated instances from four categories. It includes multi-scale targets and complex backgrounds and is therefore commonly used to evaluate remote sensing detectors. 

**HRSC2016** [19]. HRSC2016 is a ship-detection benchmark containing 1,061 images with resolutions from 300 _×_ 300 to 1500 _×_ 900 pixels. In our experiments, 436 images are used 

for training and 181 for testing. All images are resized to 800 _×_ 800 pixels before being fed into the model. 

**RTTS** [20]. RTTS is a publicly available dataset for object detection in real-world hazy scenes and is part of the RESIDE- _β_ benchmark. It contains 4,322 traffic-related images annotated in a VOC 2007-compatible format for five categories: person, bicycle, car, bus, and motorbike. Because no paired haze-free reference images are provided, RTTS is used here to evaluate detection performance under real haze rather than restoration fidelity.

## _C. Evaluation Metrics_ 

We report mean average precision (mAP), AP50, and AP75. At a fixed IoU threshold, average precision (AP) is the area under the precision–recall curve and reflects classification, localization, and confidence ranking jointly. Reporting AP at multiple IoU thresholds is useful for remote sensing detection, where small and densely distributed objects can make localization errors particularly consequential.

For evaluation, predictions are sorted by confidence and matched one-to-one with previously unmatched ground-truth boxes of the same category. The metric AP75 uses a threshold of 0.75 and is more sensitive to bounding-box accuracy. The reported mAP is the mean of AP values computed at IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95 in increments of 0.05. Together, these metrics characterize overall detection accuracy and localization quality under haze degradation.

## _D. Implementation Details_ 

All experiments are conducted on a server equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. For controlled comparisons, the evaluated variants follow the architecture and training configuration of RF-DETR Medium. The baseline uses a windowed DINOv2 visual encoder for multi-scale feature extraction. The hidden dimension of the detection branch is 256, and the feed-forward network dimension is 2048. 

The models are trained for 180 epochs using AdamW [21]. The learning rates for the main network and encoder are set to 1 _×_ 10<sup>_−_4</sup> and 1 _._ 5 _×_ 10<sup>_−_4</sup> , respectively, with a weight decay of 1 _×_ 10<sup>_−_4</sup> . The batch size is 4, and gradients are accumulated over four steps. A StepLR scheduler reduces the learning rate after epoch 100. Unless stated otherwise, the remaining settings are identical to those of the RF-DETR baseline.

## V. CONCLUSION 

This paper presents DARQ-Det, an end-to-end detector that combines degraded-input compensation, localization-focused regression, and quality-aware score calibration. DARM applies residual restoration with total variation regularization, HIFRM reweights the GIoU regression objective according to localization difficulty, and IQCM estimates box quality for candidate ranking. 

On RSOD-Haze, HRSC2016-Haze, and RTTS, DARQ-Det improves mAP over the RF-DETR baseline by 1.11, 1.16, and 0.85 percentage points, respectively. The ablation results show that each module contributes to mAP and that their combination generally provides the strongest overall performance. The gains on the real-world RTTS dataset are smaller than those on the synthetic datasets but remain consistent across mAP, AP50, and AP75.
